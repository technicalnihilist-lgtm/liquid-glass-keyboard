A real Android keyboard with a glassy, colour-shifting keypad — built as a native input method, not a web page in a shell, and fully offline.

No internet permission. Not "we don't collect data" — the app literally cannot reach the network, because it never asks for the permission. No account, no ads, no analytics, no telemetry, no update checks.

THE GLASS

Every key is layered glass rather than a flat rounded rectangle: a blurred specular rim along its top edge, an inner rim that fades to nothing at the base, a body that thins downwards, a pool of light gathering along the bottom of each key, a light source in the upper left, and two shadows — tight under the glass, wide and soft beneath — so keys sit on the sheet instead of floating above it.

On Android 12 and later the keyboard asks the system to blur whatever app is behind it, and drops its own paint to a thin veil. You type over a frosted version of the real screen, not a picture of one. Where the system cannot blur, the board falls back to its own painted backdrop.

Behind the keys, four colour orbs drift slowly and bleed through the glass. You can switch the motion off if you prefer a still board.

TYPING

- Full QWERTY with shift and double-tap caps lock
- Automatic capitals at the start of a sentence
- Numbers, symbols and emoji pages
- Long-press for accented letters: e gives è é ê ë ē ė ę, and sixteen letters carry alternates; slide to the one you want and let go
- Backspace deletes a character, repeats while held, clears a selection, and steps over whole emoji instead of splitting them
- A return key that reads the field: send, search, go, next, done or prev, and falls back to a newline in multi-line boxes
- Every press rotates the accent hue and throws a glow and a colour flare
- Popup preview above the key you are pressing
- Key-click sound and haptic tap, both switchable
- Dark glass, light glass, or follow the system theme; three key heights

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO

No predictive text, no autocorrect, no swipe typing, no clipboard history, no cloud sync. It types what you press and nothing else. That is a design decision, not a missing feature.

OPEN SOURCE

MIT licensed. The whole app is seven Java files and a handful of resources, built with aapt2, javac, d8 and apksigner — no Gradle, no dependencies, no third-party libraries. Read it, change it, build it yourself: the repository has a build script and a CI workflow that builds the APK on every push and fails if the result ever requests the internet permission.

SETTING IT UP

Install, open the app, tap "Enable keyboard", then "Choose keyboard" and pick Liquid Glass Keyboard. The app shows whether it is enabled and whether it is currently in use, and has a text box so you can try it immediately.

Requires Android 5.0 or newer. VIBRATE permission only.
